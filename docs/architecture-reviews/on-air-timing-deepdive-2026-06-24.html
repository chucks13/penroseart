On-Air Timing & the Timing Frame

Branch director-event-aware-cue-intent · 2026-06-24 · all five source files read in full · the timing-interpretation seam

The job. On-Air Timing is the one module that turns a live, untrustworthy stream of DJ rhythm data into a single, stable Timing Frame the Director can act on: "here is the beat to land your next change on, here is how confident I am, and here is why." It owns phase locking, phrase-window derivation, cue-mark planning, loop/rewind correction, and coasting.

Why it's hard. The input is the DJ playing live. Beats rewind on loops, jump forward on hot cues, phrase length changes between tracks, and Track Phase can vanish mid-song. Every fact can change every frame. This module's entire purpose is to stay coherent across those discontinuities — and that is exactly where it is strong in some places and genuinely under-defended in others.

where the input comes from

Built from BeatManager.Phrase, which reads the DJ's RaveSystem phraseState over OSC: active (in-phrase / present / absent), countBeats → beats until the next phrase boundary, lengthBeats, remaining. This geometry is re-broadcast by the DJ's gear every frame and can change discontinuously.

the split-brain caveat

Timing memory is shared: On-Air Timing owns the phase anchor + cue-sheet cursor; the Director owns lastCueBeat/lastChangeBeat and passes them in as PassLocalTimingState. On a rewind, On-Air Timing corrects the Director's values and hands them back. Two owners of one cadence fact — a real source of past regressions (see §8).

Subtle & worth confirming: a track that has a beat clock but no Track Phase (active < 0) never produces a 16-beat grid — it goes Coast (if previously anchored) or Unlocked. The PhaseClockGrid source only appears as a fallback inside the "Track Phase present but no derivable window" path. So the system fundamentally needs Track Phase to lock; raw beats alone won't sustain sequencing. Confirm that matches what RaveSystem actually always sends.

4 · Phase locking — the self-healing part

PhaseClock.Resolve is stateless and recomputed every frame. That's its great strength: when the DJ's data is internally consistent, the phase lock re-derives itself correctly each frame regardless of history — no drift to accumulate.

confidence ladder (strongest first)

  • Structural — beat + active phrase + length. phraseStart = beat − (length − countdown); the one is pinned to live phrase data.
  • Closed — beat + total beats. Grid recovered from track end: offset = total mod 16.
  • Open — beat only. Assumes beat 1 is the one (offset = 0).
  • Provisional — bar position only. A guess.
  • Unlocked — nothing usable.

built-in sanity checks

  • Good: elapsedInPhrase < 0 (countdown > length — impossible data) returns Unavailable for that frame rather than computing garbage.
  • Good: BeatInBarAgrees cross-checks the computed bar position against the DJ's reported beat-in-bar — a contradiction flag the Director/HUD can surface.
  • Cost: a single inconsistent frame (common mid-jump) drops to Unavailable, which can momentarily unlock or trigger a coast. Self-heals next frame, but it's a glitch source.

5 · Cue-sheet lifecycle — the most complex sub-area

A sheet is a phrase-relative list of marks. The hard part is keeping a current sheet and a pre-planned upcoming sheet coherent as the DJ crosses a phrase boundary, while a cue may already be in flight.

Right: same-length phrase reuse keeps the same random marks (no reroll); different length regenerates. Deterministic within a length.

The headline gap: the 3–15 beat loop dead zone.

Loop detection fires only when the backward jump is ≥ minimumChangeCadenceBeats (16). A 4- or 8-beat loop — one of the most common things a DJ does — falls in a dead band: the cursor keeps its forward position, pass-local cue/cadence memory is never cleared, and the phase math (stateless) keeps re-pinning correctly while the cue scheduling state silently drifts out of step with where the beat actually is. There is no test anywhere in the 3–15 range. This is the most likely source of "it cued in the wrong place during a loop."

7 · What's right — credit where due

Narrow seam, deep module. One ReadFrame call hides a large, gnarly implementation. The interface is far smaller than the implementation — textbook depth.
Stateless phase math. Re-derived every frame, so it self-heals and never drifts. The right call for hostile input.
Invents nothing. When it can't lock, it returns Unlocked with no fake target rather than guessing — the correct failure posture.
Provenance is observable. Source, Reanchored, IsCoasting, ClearedPassLocalState all surface why a frame decided what it did — invaluable for the Observatory and for debugging exactly this class of problem.
Deterministic seam. randomRange is injected, so cue-mark selection is fully testable without Unity.
Real loop/coast machinery exists. The ≥16 rewind path, coast, and reanchor are genuine, working, and tested — the hard cases were taken seriously.

8 · What's not — ranked by risk

  1. severe

    3–15 beat loop dead zone. Sub-16-beat loops aren't corrected and aren't tested. Most likely cause of mid-loop mis-cueing. Needs an explicit decision: detect any backward jump (not just ≥16), or define the band's behavior on purpose.

  2. high

    Split ownership of pass-local cadence. The Director holds lastCueBeat/lastChangeBeat; On-Air Timing mutates and returns them. Two owners of one fact across a seam — fragile, and historically the source of the regressions you've been fighting. Candidate to deepen: let On-Air Timing own pass-local state outright.

  3. high

    Forward jumps untested. Hot-cue jumps forward have no dedicated path or test. Stale pass-local cadence could block or mis-time the first post-jump cue.

  4. medium

    Coast assumes a 16-beat grid. Coasting steps by the cadence minimum, not the actual phrase length, so during a 32/64-beat phrase dropout the coasted marks can sit off the true grid until reanchor.

  5. low

    Readability at the turnover. The A != B || A != C guard and two PromoteUpcoming overloads concentrate accidental complexity exactly where the logic is hardest to verify by eye.

  6. low

    Beat-without-Track-Phase = Unlocked. Likely intentional, but it means a beat-only stream can't sustain sequencing. Worth a one-line confirmation against what RaveSystem guarantees.

9 · Test coverage map

Hand-crafted scenario tests, well-chosen — but they pin specific points, not the space between them.

verified (≈ right)

  • interior + mandatory cue-mark selection · gap fill · cadence
  • same-length reuse · different-length regen
  • mandatory-boundary turnover & upcoming promotion
  • coast on dropout · reanchor on return · unlock with no prior anchor
  • rewind ≥16 · cursor rewind · pass-local clear/keep
  • 1–2 beat backstep = jitter
  • PhaseClock: Structural, Closed, beat-in-bar agreement, Unavailable

unverified (risk)

  • any backward jump of 3–15 beats (the dead zone)
  • forward jumps / hot-cue seeks
  • coast across a 32/64-beat phrase (off-grid marks)
  • PhaseClock Open & Provisional tiers
  • combined stress: rewind + coast + length-change interleaved
  • contradictory single frames (countdown > length) mid-sequence
  • no property/fuzz testing over random DJ sequences

10 · Recommendations — before touching the code

In priority order. The first two are understanding/decision tasks, not yet edits — match the deepening you'll do to a decision you've made on purpose.

  1. 1 · Decide the loop model explicitly. What should a 4/8/16-beat loop do to cue scheduling? Hold position? Re-walk? Today the answer is an accident of the 16-beat threshold. Decide it, then encode it — likely "detect any backward beat jump, classify by size."
  2. 2 · Build a sequence/property test harness. Feed scripted DJ timelines (loops of every length, forward seeks, dropouts, length changes) frame-by-frame and assert invariants: cue mark never lands behind the beat, cadence never under-spaces, lock recovers within N frames. This converts the untested space into pinned behavior and will find the dead-zone bug, not just describe it.
  3. 3 · Deepen pass-local ownership. Move lastCueBeat/lastChangeBeat entirely behind the On-Air Timing seam so there is one owner of cadence memory. Removes the split-brain class of regression.
  4. 4 · Make coast length-aware. Step the coasted anchor by the last known phrase length, not a flat 16, so dropouts over long phrases stay on-grid.
  5. 5 · Polish the turnover. Replace the double-negative guard with a named predicate and collapse the two PromoteUpcoming overloads — accidental complexity removal only, behavior-preserving, after the harness exists to prove it.
Bottom line. The module is genuinely deep and its core instincts are right — stateless re-derivation, no fake targets, observable provenance, real coast/reanchor. The risk is concentrated in one place: how it reacts to backward beat motion shorter than a phrase. That's a tractable, well-bounded target. Understand the loop model first, harness it second, then deepen.
