On-Air Timing redesign

2026-06-24 · phase-first locking · phrase-as-validator · cue eviction · this is a design-in-progress, not a finished spec

Thesis. Today one file (OnAirTiming.cs) tangles three concerns at three altitudes: phase (the 16-beat grid — paramount), phrase (song structure — a validator), and cue planning (a Director concern that shouldn't live here at all). Separate them: a reusable PhaseLock core, a PhraseTracker that rides on it, and cue planning evicted to the Director. Make phase robust by fusing multiple OSC signals under one rule — beat keeps the wheel, phrase validates, event edges re-anchor.

Read this as a map of certainty: §3 is locked, §4–6 are known facts, §7 is what we still must verify, §8 is what the grill still has to decide.

1 · The problem

OnAirTiming.cs (999 lines) does three jobs at once, and they're entangled:

  • Phase locking — "where is the one" on the 16-beat grid. Universal; every part of the runtime could use it.
  • Phrase tracking — variable-length song-structure windows and boundaries.
  • Cue planning — CueSheetPlans / CueSheetCursor live as private classes inside the timing module.

Consequences: cue scheduling (a Director concern) is welded to phase locking (a universal one); phase and phrase blur together; and the only consumer is the Director, so nothing else can ask "where's the one?" The deep-dive report (on-air-timing-deepdive-2026-06-24.html) found the concrete fallout — single-source phase trust, a 3–15 beat loop dead zone, split-brain pass-local state.

2 · Phase vs phrase — the distinction we must keep

Phase — paramount

The fixed 16-beat grid (4 bars). "Where is the one." Everything — cue marks, transitions, effect downbeats — hangs off this. If phase is wrong, everything is wrong.

Phrase — the validator

Variable-length song structure (intro / buildup / drop …), always a whole number of phases (length = N × 16). Rides on phase; its job is to cross-check, not to drive.

The invariant: "phases must be in phrase." Phase boundaries must coincide with the phrase grid. When live data violates that, the violation is not noise — it is the single most valuable diagnostic we have: the trigger that says "re-examine, something changed." We want to detect that, not paper over it.

3 · The decision arbiter LOCKED

There is no single source-of-truth clock. No live signal is trustworthy enough alone — beat can be offset, phrase detection re-segments, totals go stale. So the arbiter is a method, not a source:

The arbiter is the most recently confirmed boundary (an anchor), dead-reckoned forward by the beat and continuously cross-checked. Disagreement demotes confidence — it never silently picks a winner.

precedence ladder (authority high → low)

  1. Event-edge anchors — phrase-label change, drop active 0→1. Redefine the one. Drift-immune boundary truth.
  2. Beat continuity — dead-reckon phase forward from the anchor. The steady short-term clock.
  3. Phrase structure (countdown + length) — the validator, and the bootstrap when no anchor exists. More volatile, so below beat.
  4. total_beats mod 16 — coarse fallback grid.
  5. beat_in_bar — fine cross-check; catches off-by-a-beat.

the lock's three honest states

  • LOCKED — fresh anchor + all cross-checks agree. Director cues freely.
  • COASTING — dead-reckoning, no contradiction, no recent anchor. Confidence decays with distance.
  • CONTRADICTED — phase ≠ phrase. Hold landmark decisions; actively hunt the next anchor to re-lock.
Tie-break (locked): on CONTRADICTED with no fresh edge, the beat keeps the wheel — visuals stay on the downbeat — we flag the contradiction, lower confidence, and wait for the next anchor (label change / drop) to reconcile. Phrase raises the flag, it doesn't seize control. Because everything is powers of four, small misalignment is tolerable; we detect it, we don't panic over it. Anchors are frequent (every phrase boundary, every drop), so the misalignment window is short.

PhaseLock (core). One job: the one + confidence + lock state, fused from all signals. Reusable by anything. No cue knowledge.
PhraseTracker. Phrase windows, boundaries, labels — built on a solid phase lock so boundaries snap to grid. Validates phase. No cue planning.

The seam is one read-only OnAirReading. Phase and phrase stop being conflated because they're separate modules at separate altitudes; the cue concern leaves the timing core; and any subsystem — not just the Director — can read phase.

5 · OSC signal inventory grounded

Verified from RaveOscSnapshot.cs + the parser. RaveSystem broadcasts 17 /rave/onair/* values at 60 Hz. State structs (NamedState for phrase/energy, CountdownState for drop/fill) share one shape: active tri-state (1 = now, 0 = counting to next, −1 = unavailable), countBeats (active 1 → beats to end; active 0 → beats to start), lengthBeats, remaining.

The unlock: we currently throw away the event edges (label transitions, drop downbeats) for phase. They're the highest-authority signal precisely because they fire exactly on boundaries and are immune to the countdown drift that loops and jumps cause.

6 · Loops are in-phase — the reframe

DJ loops are quantized to powers of four (usually ≥ 4 bars). Phase position is preserved exactly when loopLength mod 16 == 0:

Loopbeats16-beat phasebar / downbeat
1 bar4rotates +4preserved
2 bars8rotates +8preserved
4 bars16preservedpreserved
8 bars32preservedpreserved
Consequence: a loop does not threaten phase — by construction it lands back in-phase (the common ≥4-bar case preserves the full grid; 1–2 bar loops still preserve the bar/downbeat). The current code treats any backward jump as a threat with a brittle 16-beat threshold — wrong frame. What a loop actually disturbs is only absolute-beat cue bookkeeping (the cursor, lastCueBeat) — and that's a cue concern we're evicting anyway. If cue bookkeeping is phase/phrase-relative, loops cost almost nothing. This retires the "3–15 beat dead zone" worry: phase survives loops; only absolute cue state ever cared.

7 · What's unknown — verify before building

  • Exact drop_state / energy_state mapping. We've read the struct shape but not confirmed how ContrivePhraseEvent turns it into the edge we'd anchor on. Verify the active 0→1 transition is cleanly detectable.
  • phrase_state.remaining semantics. Unit is unconfirmed in our code (beats? bars? phrases?). Unused today; confirm with RaveSystem before relying on it.
  • Does RaveSystem always send phrase data? Today, beat-without-Track-Phase yields Unlocked. Whether that case actually occurs on your rig decides how much the total_beats fallback matters.
  • Relative reliability: phrase detection vs beat clock on your gear. The tie-break (beat keeps the wheel) assumes beat is the steadier signal. If your rig's phrase detection is actually more reliable, we'd revisit.
  • Label vocabulary & edge frequency. How often do labels change, and are they reliable enough to be a primary re-anchor? Affects how heavily we lean on them.

8 · Open decisions — the remaining grill

Decision #1 (the arbiter) is locked. Three remain.

#2 · Where does the cue cursor live once evicted?

A standalone CuePlanner core module the Director owns and calls, or folded directly into the Director. Standalone keeps the Director thin and the cue logic independently testable; folding in is less ceremony but re-tangles the Director. Lean: standalone.

#3 · Relative vs absolute cue bookkeeping the big one

Track the cursor/cadence in phase/phrase-relative terms (offset-within-phrase) — bigger rewrite, but loops and rewinds mostly evaporate — or keep absolute beats and bolt on explicit loop-folding (smaller change, more special-casing). This is the decision that most shapes the implementation. Lean: relative, because §6 makes it nearly free.

#4 · The loop model

When a loop is detected, is it a held section (freeze cue advancement, keep performing the current move) or a repeating phrase (keep advancing cue marks within the looped region)? And: do we need explicit loop detection at all if bookkeeping is relative — or does detection only matter for the creative choice of what to do during a loop?
