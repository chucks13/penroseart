Switching model rulings

Hunter's decisions on the open questions from the 31 July switching-model audit. Every audit finding is now either a ruled decision, a small fix, or closed as impossible by design.

31 July 2026 8 rulings 2 code changes implied Branch: refactor/off-plan-sighting

The outcome in three sentences

The architecture survives untouched: no new systems, counters, lattices, or queries. Nearly every remaining audit finding closed as a decision — the wire is the only truth, cleverness is replaced by self-healing, and several "defects" were declared impossible by design.

Exactly two code changes fall out: fix the Tick sampling bug that ignores a mid-beat grid datagram, and route off-plan cues through the same scheduling path planned cues already use.

The model, in Hunter's words

  1. The Director builds a new Cue Sheet on every track load. The Switcher follows the Cue Sheet.
  2. The Switcher thinks at the beginning of a grid and decides what happens at the end of it — 16 beats, more than enough time for any runway.
  3. If the Switcher thinks it needs a new cue, it asks the Director. The Director decides. A cue is a cue — off-plan or planned, it lands at the boundary closing the current grid.
  4. Once a transition starts firing, it goes through its runway and its tail. No checks, no cancellation, no replanning — because loops and resets used to double-fire everything, and fire-and-forget is what makes looping safe.
  5. Effects and transitions are first-class citizens: they report what they support through the repertoire, and they know what to do with the music on their own.

The rulings

Ruling 1 Closes: seam-source question

The wire's grid is the wall's only grid.

The on-air timing_grid is a source of truth sent by RaveSystem, which knows and understands what is happening on the network. The wall never re-derives a grid from beat plus structure, and never queries the plan for boundaries. Multiple systems reinventing the same fact and drifting out of sync is exactly how the codebase got into trouble before.

Rejected: deriving the grid from /beat + structure; retaining a boundary list on the Cue Sheet; any second grid tracker.

Ruling 2 Closes: audit findings 1 & 9 Code change

Late observation self-heals; no compensation machinery.

If a grid datagram is milliseconds late — or even half a second late for some unknown reason — who cares: it self-heals. A late think is a correct think, and lapsed marks lapse. The one real defect is ours, not the wire's: Switcher.Tick checks the grid only on the first frame of each new beat, so a grid update landing mid-beat is ignored. That gets fixed so the grid lane is observed on every frame.

Rejected: seam-repair heuristics, retry logic, any "was that really the seam" detection.

Ruling 3 Closes: audit finding 8

Think at the grid's start decides the grid's end; the ≤16-beat window is "this grid."

Cue marks happen at boundaries. At the beginning of a grid the Switcher checks what should happen at the end of it — at most 16 beats away, more than enough time for a large runway (runway ≤ 12). No boundary lookup, no plan query. The code already lets an early decision stand across re-thinks and already treats a runway that cannot fit its launch grid as a traced Miss, never compressed.

Accepted residual: on a short grid, an override staged after an early decision can miss that mark. Self-heals.

Ruling 4 Closes: audit finding 2 Code change

A cue is a cue — off-plan cues schedule like planned ones.

The Switcher sees it needs a cue, asks the Director at the beginning of the grid, and if the Director gives one, it lands at the boundary closing that grid — runway on this grid, tail on the next, like any other cue. The current Perform(beat, beat) raw-start branch, which begins the blend immediately with its impact landing mid-grid, gets deleted. Stillness relief gets no special immediate-start carve-out.

Ruling 5 Closes: audit finding 6

Fire-and-forget is absolute. Everything keyboard-driven is debug tooling.

Once the Switcher starts switching, the move goes to the end — the decision process continues in parallel, but the running blend is never reset or replaced by the machine. The past failure mode was checks that reset and double-fired moves during loops; those checks stay dead. Show Now, the keyboard jumps, and telnet commands are debug mechanisms: the production wall runs headless on a machine with no keyboard attached. The model's guarantees describe wire-driven automation only, and whatever the debug paths do mid-blend is immaterial to the show.

Noted, not scheduled: Hunter's instinct that a keyboard jump should behave like Hold (pin the effect under debug) — an optional debug-tool tweak, decided if ever needed.

Ruling 6 Closes: audit finding 3

No planner change for track ends; the existing machinery already covers every edge.

Every loaded track has a song structure divided into phrases; every phrase is divided into grids (the last grid of a phrase may run short — not every phrase is a power of four). If playback ever runs past the plan, the wire keeps supplying a grid (the contract's coasting state), the Switcher keeps thinking, and the stillness doorway deals fresh cues. DJs switch tracks before the end; if a track truly runs out and nothing plays, the OSC data goes to −1 and Standalone mode takes over — a mechanism that already exists.

Verification during implementation: confirm BeatManager passes a coasting grid through rather than dropping it.

Ruling 7 Closes: audit finding 4

Drops land on the first beat of their phrase. The wall ignores the rest.

That is what a drop is: it builds up, then hits, and every grid restarts at a phrase beginning. The structure-source distinctions (analyzed / fused / synthesized) and the drop_landing_beat field are RaveSystem internals — RaveSystem corrects everything before it broadcasts. The wall deliberately treats every drop as landing on its phrase's start_beat, by decision, so no future review re-flags it.

Side note for RaveSystem (not this repo): Hunter flagged that the OSC client contract over-describes analysis internals a client should never care about.

Ruling 8 Closes: audit findings 5 & 10

The repertoire is the capability contract; missing-capability scenarios cannot happen.

Effects and transitions are first-class citizens: they decide what happens and report what they support via the repertoire. The Director checks support when it sees a drop. Practically every effect supports drops and every effect will — and even without a drop-capable pick, the effect on the wall still knows what to do with the music. No loud validation, no fallback rework. Left exactly as it is.
